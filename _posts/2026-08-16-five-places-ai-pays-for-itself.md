The question we hear most isn't "should we use AI?" anymore. It's "where do we start so it actually pays off?"

Fair question — because the answer is *not* "everywhere." After enough integrations, a pattern emerges: the wins that pay for themselves fastest are the unglamorous ones. Here are the five we see over and over.

## 1. The inbox

Almost every business has someone spending 90+ minutes a day on email that follows patterns: the same questions, the same routing decisions, the same "just confirming receipt" replies. [AI integrated into the mail client](/ai-integrations.html) — drafting in your voice, summarizing threads, flagging what genuinely needs attention — routinely gives that person an hour a day back. That's six weeks a year, per person, from one integration.

## 2. Meeting notes and follow-through

Not the transcription — that's a commodity now. The value is what happens *after*: action items extracted and assigned, decisions logged where the team can find them, the follow-up email drafted before you're back at your desk, and the CRM updated without anyone touching it. The meeting didn't get shorter; everything downstream of it did.

## 3. The knowledge scavenger hunt

"Where's the current pricing sheet?" "What did we quote them last year?" "What's our policy on that?" Every one of those questions costs ten minutes of someone's attention. AI search across your actual documents — with answers that **cite the source file** so you can verify — turns the scavenger hunt into a ten-second lookup. This one also quietly de-risks your business: the answers stop living exclusively in one veteran employee's head.

## 4. Report assembly

Someone in your business spends part of every Friday copying numbers from three systems into one document that leadership skims for ninety seconds. An [agentic workflow](/agentic-workflows.html) assembles that report from the live systems and delivers it before anyone asks. The numbers are more current, the Friday is returned to real work, and nobody misses the copy-paste.

## 5. Intake and onboarding

New client paperwork, document collection, account setup, welcome sequences — processes that are 90% identical every time and error-prone precisely *because* they're boring. Automating the identical 90% and routing the exceptions to a human gets you consistency and speed at the moment a client is forming their first impression of you.

## What these five have in common

None of them required new software your team had to learn. None of them replaced anyone. All of them are **measurable** — you can count the hours before and after, which means you know whether it worked instead of hoping.
