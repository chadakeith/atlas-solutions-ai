Most small businesses we talk to already pay for Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, a help desk, and a pile of Apple devices. Then someone buys a shiny AI subscription that lives in another tab. Three weeks later nobody opens it.

The work never moved. The AI did.

If you want AI that people actually use, put it in the tools they already open every morning.

## Why a new AI app usually dies

Your team already has a rhythm. Mail, calendar, tickets, files. A new login is a habit change. Habit change is where most AI rollouts quietly fail.

We see the same pattern: a few people try the new tool, a few more paste client text into a free chatbot on their phone, and the official project stalls. You paid twice. Once for the unused product, and again in risk you cannot see.

This is not a people problem. It is a distance problem. The AI is too far from the work.

## Start with what you already own

A better first project is boring, and that is the point.

Email. Draft a reply in the same inbox, in a voice that sounds like your shop, and leave the send button with a human.

Help desk. Suggest a response from your own knowledge base. Route the ticket. Do not invent an answer from the public internet.

Documents. Ask a question across the policies and proposals you already store, and make it cite the file.

Google Workspace or Microsoft 365. Meeting notes, follow-ups, and file search inside the suite you already pay for, on the business plan that has real data controls.

Apple devices. If you already manage Macs with Jamf or Apple Business, the AI tools should follow the same identity and device rules as everything else. A chatbot that ignores your MDM is just another shadow app.

None of that requires a new platform. It requires someone who already understands your identity, permissions, and devices.

## What to refuse

Do not upload client mail, payroll, or health info into a free consumer chatbot. If a vendor cannot tell you where the data goes, skip them.

Do not connect AI to a system until you know which account it is using. Shared inboxes and leftover admin logins are how this goes wrong.

Do not skip a short written rule. A one-page [AI policy](/ai-policy.html) that says what is encouraged, what needs a second look, and what is off limits is enough to start. You can tighten it later.

## A first week that is actually a first week

Pick one inbox, one ticket queue, or one shared drive. Not five.

Decide what data is allowed in. Write that down.

Pilot with three people who do the work, not a steering committee.

If it saves them time, expand it. If it does not, kill it. That is a successful test.
